Privacy

Who sees your health data

You decide who sees each kind of data. Private medical conferences stay between you and your flight surgeon, and mission control only learns whether you are fit for duty. Your choices are saved only on this device.

Sharing by data type

Changes are saved on board right away and reach Earth at the next sync.

All changes saved on board.

In an emergency

When an emergency starts (SOS, or a reading that needs action now), your location and the vital signs of that alert are shared with the crew right away, so they can reach you. Nothing else is shared, and this does not change your choices below.

  • Vital signs

    Heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen, breathing and temperature from your Astroskin shirt.

  • Radiation dose

    Your personal dosimeter readings and the projected dose for the mission.

  • Body

    Body mass, bone density, muscle and eye exams across the mission.

  • Women's health

    Cycle plan, contraception and urinary symptoms. Only used if you turn the module on.

  • Men's health focus

    Vision check-ins, hearing tests and reproductive health notes. Only used if you turn the module on.

  • Mood and sleep log

    Your quick daily check-in about how you feel and how you slept.

  • Private conference notes

    Notes from your weekly private medical conference.

Why can't I turn some of them off?

Your flight surgeon must see vital signs and radiation dose to keep you safe in flight, and the crew medical officer needs your radiation dose to call storm-shelter time. Private conference notes are the opposite: they are never shared with anyone else.

Fit for duty

Mission control only learns whether you are fit for duty, never the medical reason. This keeps the separation NASA already uses between private medicine and mission operations.

Researchers and your consent

Only about 75 women have flown to space, so most space health data comes from men.

When you share with researchers, your name and identifiers are removed first. You can stop sharing at any time, and data you have not shared never leaves the medical team.
