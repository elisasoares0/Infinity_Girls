Your health risks

What the mission does to your body, in plain words. Each risk says what the app watches for you and what you can do about it today.

Why each exam before launch matters: it records the reference that every later check is compared with.

Your body is readapting to Earth's gravity. Here is what changes, what the app watches and what helps.

The 5 hazards of spaceflight

NASA groups the health risks of a mission under five hazards. Open a risk to see what it does, what the app watches and what you can do.

Radiation

Outside the Earth's magnetic field, particles from the Sun and from deep space pass through the hull and through your body.

  • Cancer and other effects of radiation
    What it does to your body
    Over a career, radiation raises the risk of cancer, and it can also affect the heart, the brain and fertility.
    What the app watches for you
    Your dose so far against the 600 mSv career limit, and live space weather from NASA DONKI.
    What you can do
    During a solar storm alert, go to the storm shelter and stay until the alert ends. Keep your dosimeter on you.
    See your radiation dose

Isolation and confinement

Months in a small space with the same few people, far from family and friends.

  • Mood, stress and mental health
    What it does to your body
    Isolation, a heavy workload and short sleep can lower your mood, focus and patience.
    What the app watches for you
    The daily mood check-in, and your sleep from the shirt.
    What you can do
    Answer the check-in honestly: only the medical team sees it. Talk to your flight surgeon early, and keep in touch with family when the link allows.
    See today's health tasks
  • Working together as a crew
    What it does to your body
    Small frictions grow with time and stress in a small crew, and they affect safety.
    What the app watches for you
    Not measured by the app: talk to your flight surgeon in the private medical conference.
    What you can do
    Raise small problems early, in the crew meeting or in private.
  • Sleep and body clock
    What it does to your body
    Noise, light and long workdays shift your body clock. Short nights slow your reactions the next day.
    What the app watches for you
    Your sleep, from the shirt, every night.
    What you can do
    Keep your sleep window, dim the lights 30 minutes before, and keep the sleep station dark and cool.
    See it in Vitals

Distance from Earth

Help from Earth takes time and nobody can come home quickly, so the crew handles problems on board.

  • Tools and support that fit the crew
    What it does to your body
    Hard-to-use equipment and slow answers from Earth make mistakes more likely, above all when you are tired.
    What the app watches for you
    The app itself: guides that work offline, a voice that reads each step, and an alert to the crew with your location.
    What you can do
    Read the emergency guides before you need them, and run a drill with the crew.
    Open the emergency guides
  • Food and water
    What it does to your body
    The food must last the whole mission and its vitamins fade over time. Eating or drinking too little makes you lose weight and strength.
    What the app watches for you
    The water you take from the dispenser, your blood glucose from the glucometer, and your body mass.
    What you can do
    Drink to the daily goal and eat your full menu, even when you are not hungry.
    See it in Vitals

Changes in gravity

Your body is built for Earth's gravity. With less of it, fluids, bones, muscles and balance change, and they change again when you come back.

  • Balance and motion sickness
    What it does to your body
    Your inner ear gets mixed signals when gravity changes: dizziness, motion sickness and clumsiness, above all in the first days.
    What the app watches for you
    Not measured by a sensor: tell the medical team when you feel dizzy.
    What you can do
    Move your head slowly in the first days after each change of gravity, and avoid critical tasks while dizzy.
  • Heart and blood vessels
    What it does to your body
    Fluids move toward your head and the heart works less. Back in gravity, you can feel faint when you stand up.
    What the app watches for you
    Heart rate and blood pressure from the shirt, all day.
    What you can do
    Keep your exercise sessions and drink enough water: both keep the heart and vessels ready for gravity.
    See it in Vitals
  • Bones
    What it does to your body
    Bones that carry less weight lose density, mostly in the hips and spine.
    What the app watches for you
    An estimate of your bone loss, and the bone scans before and after the mission.
    What you can do
    Do your resistance exercise every day: it is the best protection your bones have.
    See your bones and muscles
  • Muscles and fitness
    What it does to your body
    Muscles and fitness fade fast when they work against less weight.
    What the app watches for you
    Your exercise minutes, logged by the exercise devices.
    What you can do
    Reach the daily exercise goal. The devices log it on their own.
    See your bones and muscles
  • Eyes and vision (SANS)
    What it does to your body
    Fluid shifting toward the head can swell the back of the eye and blur near vision. It is NASA's top health risk for long missions.
    What the app watches for you
    Your retina scans (OCT) during the mission.
    What you can do
    Tell the medical team about any blurred vision, and keep every retina scan on your task list.
    See your eye scans See today's health tasks
  • Launch and landing forces
    What it does to your body
    Strong shaking and g-forces at launch and landing can strain the neck and back.
    What the app watches for you
    Not measured by the app: report any pain after launch or landing.
    What you can do
    Keep the seat fit and the posture you trained, and report pain early.

A closed and hostile environment

The sealed cabin keeps you alive, but its air, its microbes and the spacesuit itself bring risks of their own.

  • Immune system
    What it does to your body
    Stress, radiation and short sleep weaken your defenses, and viruses that slept in your body can wake up.
    What the app watches for you
    Your temperature from the shirt, and the weekly saliva and blood samples: cortisol, ketones and the immune marker.
    What you can do
    Do the saliva collection, wash your hands before meals, and report a fever early.
    See it in Vitals See today's health tasks
  • Microbes in the cabin
    What it does to your body
    Bacteria and fungi grow in a closed habitat, and some change in space.
    What the app watches for you
    Not measured by the app beyond your symptoms.
    What you can do
    Clean surfaces and the water system on schedule, and report symptoms early.
    See your urinary symptoms log
  • Oxygen and cabin air
    What it does to your body
    Too little oxygen or too much carbon dioxide (CO₂) in the air causes headache, tiredness and slow thinking.
    What the app watches for you
    Your blood oxygen (SpO₂) and breathing rate from the shirt.
    What you can do
    With a headache and fast breathing, check the cabin air alarm and tell the crew.
    See it in Vitals
  • Decompression sickness
    What it does to your body
    Going from cabin pressure to the low pressure of the suit can form gas bubbles in the blood: joint pain, numbness or a rash.
    What the app watches for you
    Not measured by the app: the suit team logs the pre-breathe before each spacewalk.
    What you can do
    Do the full pre-breathe before each spacewalk and report joint pain at once.
  • Injuries in the spacesuit
    What it does to your body
    Working in a stiff, pressurized suit strains hands, shoulders and knees. On the Moon and Mars, falls and dust add to it: dust irritates the eyes and lungs.
    What the app watches for you
    Your readiness, which says whether you are cleared for a spacewalk today.
    What you can do
    Stop and report pain, rest your hands between spacewalks, and clean dust off the suit seals before you come in.
    See your readiness

Why each exam before launch

Your flight surgeon schedules them. Each one is the reference for a later check.

  • Annual flight certification exam

    Confirms you are fit for the mission and records your reference values.

  • Hearing test (audiogram)

    Your reference before months of noise: any later change is compared with it.

  • Baseline retina scan (OCT)

    Swelling at the back of the eye (SANS) is found by comparing later scans with this one.

  • Blood, urine and saliva samples

    Reference for your immune system and metabolism.

  • Hip structure scan (QCT)

    3D hip strength, to measure bone loss on missions of 6 months or more.

  • Baseline bone scan (DXA)

    Bone density reference, compared with the scan 5 days after landing.

  • Private medical conference

    A private talk with your flight surgeon about anything that worries you.

  • Pregnancy test

    Required in the last 10 days before launch. Only its status and date are shown, never the result.

  • Fitness training

    Your training goal builds the strength and fitness that the exercise program keeps later.

Readapting to gravity

Open each one to see what it does, what the app watches and what helps.

  • Feeling faint when you stand up
    What it does to your body
    Your heart and vessels relearn to push blood up against gravity. The first days you can feel dizzy or faint when you stand.
    What the app watches for you
    Heart rate and blood pressure from the shirt.
    What you can do
    Stand up slowly, sit down at once if you feel faint, and drink water through the day.
    See it in Vitals
  • Balance and walking
    What it does to your body
    Walking, turning your head and driving feel off for days to weeks while the inner ear readapts.
    What the app watches for you
    Not measured by a sensor: your rehab team tests your balance.
    What you can do
    Do not drive until you are cleared, hold the handrails, and follow the rehab plan step by step.
    See today's health tasks
  • Bones
    What it does to your body
    Bone comes back slowly, over months to years. The scan 5 days after landing is compared with your scan before the flight.
    What the app watches for you
    Your bone scans and the estimate on the Body page.
    What you can do
    Keep the resistance exercise of the rehab plan, and get enough calcium and vitamin D.
    See your bones and muscles
  • Muscles and fitness
    What it does to your body
    Strength and fitness come back with exercise, faster than bone.
    What the app watches for you
    Your rehab exercise minutes, logged by the devices.
    What you can do
    Reach the daily rehab goal and rest when your body asks.
    See your bones and muscles
  • Eyes and vision
    What it does to your body
    Eye changes are checked with a retina scan after landing. Most of them improve with time.
    What the app watches for you
    Your retina scans compared with the ones before the flight.
    What you can do
    Tell the medical team about any blurred vision.
    See your eye scans
